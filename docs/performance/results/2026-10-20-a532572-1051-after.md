# #1051 before and after PR #1139, 2026-10-20

**Snapshot of two runs on 2026-10-20, not refreshing.** Two `npm run perf:compare` runs, 10 repetitions each, medium and large, 1x and 4x, on the owner's PC with GPU Chromium 149 on the RTX 3060: BEFORE is main at `06a6dcb` (17:20Z to 18:55Z), AFTER is `a532572`, the measured commit of PR #1139 (18:55Z to 20:28Z). Live numbers come from re-running `npm run perf:compare`. Raw data: [AFTER](https://github.com/maniator/verticopolis-benchmarks/tree/main/results/2026-10-20-a532572-1051-after) and [BEFORE](https://github.com/maniator/verticopolis-benchmarks/tree/main/results/2026-10-20-06a6dcb-1051-before) (private repository).

The pair is evidence for #1139 as an optimization of the Rust engine, not the cross-engine acceptance of #1051. Large-tower cross-engine ratios are invalid in both runs (the page had already run medium, #1162, fixed by #1164); within-engine before and after rows are valid. Both runs predate the `gpuVerified` field.

Hosted main-thread WASM (the app's host), medium at 1x: 2,341 ms before and 2,128 ms after, against TypeScript at 818 ms. Medium frame p99 at 1x: WASM 45.27 ms before and 44.79 ms after, against TypeScript's 29 ms.

## What it shows

- The bare WASM engine got faster in every row (large 1x from 822 to 296 ms), and TypeScript, the drift control, held still within 5%.
- Hosted WASM improved only a little and still took about 2.6 to 2.9 times TypeScript's time on medium; the cost was host-side sync (74.6% of hosted time on large at 1x). Frame pacing on medium still failed.
- #1051 stayed open for the hosted and frame criteria. The fair large-tower read is [2026-10-21-9cd44d1-1051-fresh-large.md](2026-10-21-9cd44d1-1051-fresh-large.md).
