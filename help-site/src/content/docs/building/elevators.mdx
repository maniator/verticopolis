If a tower has a heart, it's the elevators. Every office worker, resident, guest and shopper above the ground floor rides one, and when the elevators can't keep up, nothing else matters: people wait, then grumble, then leave. Most of the art of a good tower is the art of moving people.

## Placing a shaft

Choose an elevator and drag from its bottom floor to its top floor. The shaft costs its base price plus a little for every floor it spans past the first. You can make it taller or shorter later from its card.

To stretch a shaft, click it to pick it, then drag the arrow at its top or bottom. The shaft grows as far as it can and stops on the last floor it may serve: a floor where another shaft stands, or a lobby floor (the ground floor, or a sky lobby) where the lobby under the shaft isn't built yet. When you let go, the game tells you why it stopped. Build the lobby across the shaft first, then drag again to carry it through.

A shaft comes with one car. Add more from the card, up to **eight**: each extra car costs $40,000, and you get half of that back if you take it off. Every car also costs a small sum in upkeep each month.

## Express elevators and sky lobbies

The standard elevator's limit of 30 floors is no accident. A tall tower is meant to be built in stages: an express shaft carries people from the ground lobby straight up to a [sky lobby](/help/building/floors-and-lobbies/), and from there standard elevators take them the last few floors. An express never stops between lobbies, which is what makes it fast, and it holds twice as many people as a standard car.

<span class="vc-mode">Classic</span> As in 1994, an express has to reach the ground floor or a 15th floor (15, 30, 45 and so on). Drag from one of those floors; the shaft can end anywhere, but an end that is not on one of them is only passed, never stopped at.

<span class="vc-mode">Classic</span> Every car moves at the same speed, express included, as in 1994.

<span class="vc-mode">Modern</span> Cars move twice as fast as in Classic, and an express moves twice as fast again, so office workers who ride in each morning get to their desks on time.

A tower that makes people change elevators three or four times on the way home will crawl. People will ride as many elevators as it takes, but every change is another wait.

## Stops

Inspect a standard elevator to see its stops. You can turn off a floor the shaft passes, so it runs past without stopping; that is useful for a shaft meant to serve only the top of its run. Anyone already riding to a floor you turn off stays aboard, gets off at the nearest stop and finds the rest of the way from there, by stairs or another shaft. With no other way on, they head for the lobby and leave. Housekeepers keep to the service elevators and stairs. An express stops at every basement floor it passes, as in the original.

<span class="vc-mode">Classic</span> An express also stops at the ground floor and at every 15th floor it reaches, whether or not a sky lobby is built there yet, just as the 1994 game does. You can turn any of those stops off and back on in the elevator's **Schedule** window; no other floor can be turned on. A tower saved by an earlier version, when an express in Classic stopped only at lobbies, loads with its 15th floors stopping again and keeps any stop you had turned off.

<span class="vc-mode">Modern</span> Above the ground an express serves only lobbies and its two ends, and when you build a new sky lobby on a floor it passes, it starts stopping there by itself. You can turn a basement stop off and back on in the **Schedule** window.

A saved tower keeps your choices. A tower saved by an earlier version, when an express skipped the basements, loads with every basement stop on.

## Schedules

Each shaft has a **schedule**: how many of its cars run in each hour of a weekday and of a weekend, which floor each car goes home to when it's idle, and how long a car waits at a floor before it leaves. The default works for most shafts. When a shaft is swamped at nine and idle at noon, the schedule is where to fix it. When two shafts of the same kind serve the same floors, riders split between them by the cars each one has running, so a shaft you cut to one car for the hour gets a one-car share.

A car you add joins only the hours that already run every car. An hour you cut back keeps its count, and so does one you cut back before you sold a car, so taking a car off and adding it again never puts the whole fleet back on your quiet hours. Pressing OK in the Schedule window keeps that; setting the hour to a new count is what changes it.

<span class="vc-mode">Classic</span> The schedule is the raw hour-by-hour grid, set by hand, as in 1994.

<span class="vc-mode">Modern</span> The schedule also offers presets for common jobs, a one-tap auto-tune from the waits it has measured (each day type needs six hours of watching first, and a quiet hour counts; a shaft that people avoid because it runs few cars is tuned from the busier shafts that stop at the same floors, so it can win its cars back; each hour gets enough cars to carry its riders with room, and an hour whose few cars ran full gets one more car each time you tune), and advice on which hours the shaft has too many or too few cars running.

<span class="vc-mode">Modern</span> The **Auto** box in the Schedule window keeps a shaft tuned for you. While it is checked, the shaft re-tunes its car counts early every morning from the traffic it measured, the same way the auto-tune button does, so a tower that grows or changes keeps its elevators in step without a visit. Auto sets only the counts: the home floors, the stops and the waiting times stay as you set them. Until a day type has been measured, and for any hour the shaft hasn't watched yet, the count stays as it was. While Auto is on, the presets and the hour-by-hour counts are locked; uncheck Auto to set them by hand again. A new elevator starts with Auto checked. A tower saved by an earlier version loads with Auto unchecked on every shaft, so its schedules stay exactly as you left them.

An elevator's card shows its **Avg load**, how full its cars have run lately. It counts everyone aboard once: the people you can see riding and the riders the game only estimates.

<span class="vc-mode">Modern</span> The schedule's advice, the auto-tune button and the **Auto** box count riders the same way.

## Keeping the waits short

The game measures how long people actually wait. When a floor's people wait too long on average, the floor counts as jammed, and the homes, offices and hotel rooms on it lose rating every hour until it clears, and so do the shops on it. A floor with only shops, restaurants, clinics and other venues on it counts only the last hour's waits, so once its customers stop waiting long, it clears at the next hour. At nine in the morning, if the whole tower is badly backed up, tenants will tell you so.

The Map's **Traffic** view colors each floor Clear, Busy or Jammed. When a floor turns red:

1. **Add cars** to the shafts that serve it, up to eight each.
2. **Add a shaft.** Two shafts side by side share the load.
3. **Shorten trips.** Add an express to a sky lobby, so long trips skip the local stops.
4. **Check the schedule** for hours with too few cars running.

A tenant who complains of crowded elevators names the shaft on their floor with the most room, such as "the standard elevator on floors 1–15 runs 3 of 8 cars", so you can add cars there first. A car costs less than a new shaft and takes no room on the floors. When every elevator that stops on the floor already runs eight cars, the tenant says so, and a parallel shaft is the next step. Service elevators carry only staff, so they are never the one named.

People get on only a car that is going their way. A car that stops at a floor shows which way it leaves, so someone bound up lets a car heading down go by and waits for the next one up, as they would in a real building. A car on its way down passes a floor where everyone waiting wants to go up, unless that floor is where it turns around.

<span class="vc-mode">Modern</span> People are a little more patient: they will wait half as long again before a floor counts as jammed.

<span class="vc-mode">Modern</span> A full car doesn't stop for people it has no room for. It passes their floor and stops only where someone aboard gets off, and another car of the shaft comes for them.

During a bomb search every car stops where it is, and the riders and the people at the landings wait with it until the guards are done. That time does not count against your waits. You can't add or take away cars, stretch a shaft, or change its stops or schedule until the search ends. See [Events](/help/tower/events/#bomb-threats).
