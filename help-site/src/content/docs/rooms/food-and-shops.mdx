Shops and restaurants don't bring people into your tower. They earn from the people who are already there. An office worker grabbing lunch, a hotel guest out for dinner, a family picking up groceries on the way home: that is where a shop's money comes from. Build them for a crowd you already have, and they pay handsomely. Build them in an empty tower and they sit idle.

## How a shop earns

Every hour a shop is open and people can reach it, it takes in a share of the tower's spending. Roughly:

- Each **office worker** and each **hotel guest** is good for about $30 a day.
- Each **resident** of a condo or apartment spends about a third of that.
- A working [Metro station](/help/rooms/services/) brings in visitors from outside and adds a quarter on top. Plenty of [recycling](/help/rooms/services/) adds a little more.

The shops in reach split that spending between them by how much each can serve. Too many shops for too few people, and each gets a thin slice.

A few things change a day's takings:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| **Far from a lobby** | A shop more than two floors from a lobby or sky lobby takes half as much. Keep shops near lobbies. |
| **Rain** | About one day in seven it rains, and fewer people go out: takings fall by half (by less with a Metro), and fast food suffers most. |
| **Weekends** | In Classic, restaurants, fast food and shops all do better on the weekend. In Modern it depends on the kind: restaurants, bars and boutiques do better, fast food and the daycare do worse. |
| **Open hours** | A shop only earns while it is open. Each one's hours are in its fact box. |

The shop's card shows how many customers came and what it made yesterday, so you can see which ones pull their weight.

<span class="vc-mode">Classic</span> As in the 1994 game, a new fast food, restaurant or shop counts 10 visitors on its first day, and the count climbs by 5 to 15 a day as word gets around, until it tops out (a little higher on weekends). Those visitors count toward your population.

## Noise

Shops and restaurants are noisy. Offices within 11 tiles on the same floor hear them, and homes and hotel rooms within 21 tiles. A lobby between them stops the noise. The best place for a row of shops is a lobby floor or the floor next to one, with no homes on it.

## When there aren't enough shops

Office workers, residents and hotel guests all want somewhere to eat and shop that they can get to. If the shops they can reach could serve fewer than half of them, their ratings are held down. In Modern, a cinema, party hall or aquatic center they can reach counts toward that too, though seats can't replace shops altogether. See [Tenant ratings](/help/tower/ratings/).
