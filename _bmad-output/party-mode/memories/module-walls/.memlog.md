- (note) MEASURED from a real SimTower render: floor pitch is exactly 36px (nine consecutive gaps of 36), elevator shaft 31-32px wide = 4 tiles x 8px. Original geometry is TILE=8, FLOOR=36, ratio 4.5:1.
- (note) Verticopolis is TILE=11, FLOOR=4*TILE=44, ratio 4.0:1 (src/render/scale.ts). Its comment justifies 4:1 as making the 4-tile car 'read square, as in the 1994 original' - but the original's 4-tile car is 32x36, NOT square. The stated rationale does not match the measurement.
- (constraint) TRANSPORT_BAND_FLOORS=45 is pinned so a shaft bitmap (45*FLOOR) stays under a 2048px GPU texture cap: at FLOOR=44 that is 1980px. Any FLOOR increase past ~45 forces the band count down.
- (note) Candidate ratio fixes: TILE=10/FLOOR=45 (exact 4.5:1, 1.25x original, small move from 11/44, 45*45=2025 still under the cap); TILE=8/FLOOR=36 (exact original, resolution downgrade); TILE=16/FLOOR=72 (exact 2x original, but 45*72=3240 breaks the texture cap).
- (note) Readability problem as observed: a floor of offices in Verticopolis reads as one continuous band of desks with no separator; the original bounds each module visibly and furnishes it (chairs, plants, pictures, a door), so a floor reads as a row of discrete rooms.
- (note) MEASURED palette comparison on office floors: original uses 43 distinct colors (4-bit bins) vs ours 188 (4.4x looser). Original avg luminance 103.5 / saturation 0.272; ours 126.2 / 0.366 (brighter and 34 percent more saturated).
- (note) KEY: the original's darkest decile sits at luminance 18 (true near-black); ours bottoms out at 37. The original's module seams read BECAUSE it keeps true blacks between rooms. This links the divider problem to the palette problem: one fix serves both.
- (assumption) Palette sample caveat: the original capture is daylight and ours is night (00:04), and our region includes a translucent overlay band, so luminance/saturation are not perfectly comparable. The 43-vs-188 color-count gap is robust to that; the brightness gap should be re-measured on a daylight capture before acting on it.
- (note) DAYLIGHT re-measure (interiors only, no sky, clear of the overlay column; ours fast-forwarded to WD2 12:04): original 43 colors / avgLum 103.5 / sat 0.272 / p10 18 / p50 116; ours 99 colors / avgLum 166.5 / sat 0.358 / p10 97 / p50 167.
- (decision) The night-shot color count of 188 was inflated by sky; the honest gap is 99 vs 43 (2.3x). The brightness hypothesis is CONFIRMED and larger than suspected: ours is 60 percent brighter overall.
- (decision) HEADLINE: our daylight interiors contain no dark values at all. Darkest decile 97 vs the original's 18, a 79-point gap. There is literally no dark tone available to draw a module seam with, which is why dividers do not read. Restoring a true dark end to the interior ramp is the root fix.
- (note) MEASURED texture density (daylight interiors): original edgeDensity 0.233, ditherIndex 0.039, avg flat run 4.29px; ours 0.138 / 0.019 / 7.27px. The original packs MORE detail into FEWER colors; ours has more colors but larger flat areas.
- (decision) This inverts the naive reading of Finding 2. The original is not 'pixel art' as a style, it is constrained-palette art straining toward realism via dithering and dense shading. Ours is deliberate flat pixel art. The gap the owner feels as 'more real' is spatial frequency, not color count.
- (question) Art direction fork raised by the owner: chase the original's rendered-miniature realism, keep Verticopolis's flat pixel-art identity, or take a middle ground (tight palette + dark anchor + denser interior shading, without dithering)? This decides what the palette work in Finding 2 is aiming AT.
