The Issue-Template Roundtable

6 July 2026 · sparked by a bug form pointing reporters at a save file the game never makes
Two forms. Two wrong doors. The fix wasn't more words — it was making the right door look like a door.
The bug form named a file that doesn't exist; the feature form whispered a redirect the parity form shouted. Symmetry, not verbosity.

Round 1 · Should the parity report ask for a .vctower?

🎨
SallyUX Designer
Who's filing a parity report? Someone already annoyed, who counted something in a 1994 video. Now I add an attach-a-file step? On mobile? That's where forms go to die.
📋
JohnProduct Manager
What's the job? Convince us a number is wrong. Half our "cannot reproduce" closes are because we can't recreate their exact tower. The .vctower isn't friction — it's the thing that makes the claim reproducible.
🏗️
WinstonSystem Architect
But a divergence is often structural, not positional. "Express elevators counted in the wrong pool" — I need one express elevator, not your save. The canon lives in one file.
📊
MaryBusiness Analyst
That's the clue, Winston. Sometimes it's "at 42 floors with this tenant mix, rent settles where the original never would." That you can't verify from the rule alone. The save is gold for economy and population — near-useless for a build-cap rule. The answer's conditional.
📚
PaigeTechnical Writer
You're all fighting over a field nobody proposed right. The bug form didn't add a required upload — it put five words inside an existing field. "if relevant." A hint, not a hurdle. Do the same here.
✔ Landed
An inline hint in the Current Verticopolis behavior field: "for economy or population divergences, attach your saved tower export (.vctower) so we can reproduce the exact state." No new field, no required flag. It self-sorts — build-cap reporters ignore it, rent reporters get the nudge.

Round 2 · Should the feature form warn "not for parity"?

📋
JohnProduct Manager
Read what's already there. Line 12 already redirects to the parity template. The question isn't "add it" — it's "why is it ignored?"
🎨
SallyUX Designer
Because it's the last sentence of a five-line paragraph about modes. Nobody reads paragraph four. It's placed to be missed.
📊
MaryBusiness Analyst
Here's the tell — the asymmetry. The parity form has a bouncer: a loud ⚠️ "don't file Modern differences here." The feature form has a sticky note. Reporters route wrong in exactly the direction we made quiet.
📚
PaigeTechnical Writer
Same information, no visual signal. The eye doesn't stop where nothing tells it to stop. Give it the same ⚠️ callout the parity form uses. Symmetry — each form points firmly at the other.
🏗️
WinstonSystem Architect
A structural fix, not a rewrite. Pull the redirect out of the mode paragraph, give it its own line. Boring, consistent, done.
✔ Landed
The parity redirect extracted into its own ⚠️ callout — "This form is for behavior we don't have yet." — mirroring the parity form's own warning. The two templates now guard each other's doors with equal weight.

Where the room actually clashed

Attach the save

John & Mary: reproducibility is the whole job; economy/population math needs the exact state or we close it "cannot reproduce."

Don't tax the form

Sally & Winston: most divergences are structural — one elevator proves them. A required upload is pure friction on the majority case.

  • The synthesis nobody walked in with: Paige's "hint in an existing field, if relevant" dissolved the fight — it's invisible to the people it doesn't help and a lifeline to the people it does.
  • The diagnosis that reframed round 2: the redirect wasn't missing, it was outranked by its own neighbor. Mary's asymmetry catch — one door with a bouncer, one with a sticky note — was the real bug.
  • Running joke: "Converged again. We should stop meeting like this." — three unanimous verdicts in one session, which Winston was told to mark on the calendar.
